% Design, Fig. C (Section 3.3): runtime re-sectoring at the boundary of W_k and W_k+1 on the running example.
% (1) A sample of the live terms at the start of W_k, a share from every node (moved through the Clifford operations of
% W_k; branching is ignored). (2) The Row Solver computes the rows that every gate of W_k+1 conserves (the GF(2) kernel
% of its generators); x1, a row of W_k, is not among them, since X1 flips it. (3) The Sector Balancer draws candidate
% row pairs from the kernel, counts the sample in each sector, and keeps the pair whose largest sector is smallest; the
% number of rows g follows the expected terms per sector and the GPU count. (4) The rows go to the split on every GPU;
% sector d of W_k+1 belongs to node d mod N. All of this runs on the CPU while the GPUs apply W_k.
