This paper presents \SectorQ, a distributed Pauli propagation system for observable simulation of quantum circuits with more than 100 qubits on GPU clusters.
Lower coefficient cutoffs increase the retained expansion and its memory requirements, while gate-level global merges impose coordination costs on distributed GPU propagation.
\SectorQ constructs window-conserved parity sectors from the generators of consecutive gates, assigning all contributions to an equal Pauli string to one sector and permitting local merge and truncation throughout each window.
The runtime selects conserved rows using sampled live terms during GPU execution and stores sectors in distributed host memory, with one sector loaded per GPU worker and term exchange restricted to window boundaries.
An evaluation of seven circuits on up to 512 NVIDIA A100 GPUs shows 4.4-9.7$\times$ speedups over original-circuit \cuPP at the largest common settings on 16 GPUs and a 140$\times$ larger completed term population on 512 GPUs.
